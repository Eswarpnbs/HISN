# Cross-border essential message (gap G7)

_Does essential-sender recognition survive a border? Realistic senders: a home-country public-health authority or an embassy reaching its nationals on local numbers (how often this happens is not documented). Synthetic, fictional senders; a person on a UAE number reached through an international A2P route (roaming not modelled). Domain-level finding, not a national recommendation._

## At the origin (India): a legal finding
A government message is sent only via DLT and is identifiable by its -G header suffix: **IN-TRAI-GOVMSG** (Regulation 2, definition (za): Government Message (as amended 2025)), [official source](https://www.trai.gov.in/sites/default/files/2026-05/CA_21052026.pdf). This protects it from customer opt-outs (the Preference Register); protection from operator AI filtering is not addressed.

## At the destination (UAE): measured in HISN's UAE firewall
The only sender-identity rule is the marketing label: **UAE-TDRA-UEC-8.8** (Clause 8.8 (marketing sender label AD-XXXX)), [official source](https://tdra.gov.ae/-/media/About/regulations-and-ruling/EN/cellular-phone-spam-regulatory-policy-English.ashx). The same critical message (INJ-CRITLAB) was run from the registered domestic hospital and from the Indian essential sender `IN-HLTHGV-G`. Note that a genuine foreign essential sender arrives the same way as a spoofed one (foreign route, unauthenticated, unknown to the registry):

| Condition | Domestic registered hospital | Indian essential sender |
|---|---|---|
| Current rules, normal day | ALLOW | **ALLOW** |
| Current rules, after the panic threshold (0.20) | QUARANTINE | **BLOCK_OVERRIDABLE** |
| Tested rule set, after the panic threshold (0.20) | ALLOW | **BLOCK_OVERRIDABLE** |

## Finding
Recognition stops at the border: under conditions where the domestic essential sender is delivered, the foreign essential sender is not. Sender authentication fixes this only for senders the destination country knows; essential messages need recognition across borders.
After the panic threshold, the foreign message is blocked outright while the domestic one is held for a human to review.

Global example (partial): Singapore's Sender ID Registry admits overseas senders only through a Singapore-registered entity, and its operator states the protection applies to Singapore mobile numbers only ([IMDA](https://www.imda.gov.sg/How-We-Can-Help/Anti-Scam-Measures), [SGNIC FAQ](https://www.sgnic.sg/faq/sms-sender-id-registry)). Protection is national by design; no mutual recognition was found.
